JOB MARKET PAPER

Is the Mean Enough? The Distribution of Inflation Expectations and the Aggregate Phillips Curve

Using individual survey responses from 1990 to 2025, I ask whether the mean inflation expectation is enough to explain inflation dynamics or whether the distribution of beliefs also matters.

Read abstract

The representative-agent New Keynesian Phillips Curve summarizes inflation expectations by their mean, reducing the cross-sectional distribution of beliefs to a single point forecast. Using individual responses from the University of Michigan Survey of Consumers from 1990 to 2025, I compare the mean with dispersion, directional shifts, and tail risk. Accounting for these distributional features, particularly dispersion and tail risk in the full sample, produces a steeper aggregate Phillips Curve, while the mean expectation becomes insignificant. During COVID, changes in the distribution begin in the tails before developing into a rightward shift. During the Great Recession, a leftward shift occurs before the lower tail expands. The distribution therefore contains information beyond its mean.

Presented at the Federal Reserve Banks of Philadelphia and Cleveland. Upcoming presentations: Illinois Economic Association (October 2026), Southern Economic Association (November 2026), and Midwest Macroeconomics Conference (November 2026).

WORKING PAPER

When the Poor Pay More for Groceries: Online Retail Transformation and Monetary Policy

With Xudong An and Rodney Ramcharan

Using 100 million grocery transactions from 228,519 U.S. households, we find that low-income households experienced 1.4% more grocery price inflation than high-income households in the post-COVID period. We examine the roles of online shopping, warehouse clubs, and monetary policy.

WORKING PAPER

From Regional to Aggregate: Trade Openness and the Phillips Curve Break After 2000

I examine whether trade exposure contributed to the flattening of the U.S. Phillips curve after 2000 using regional data and a shift-share approach.
