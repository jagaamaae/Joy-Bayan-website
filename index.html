02 / RESEARCH

Research

My research connects household behavior, markets, and macroeconomic policy across several settings.

JOB MARKET PAPER

A Heterogeneous-Beliefs Phillips Curve: Evidence from Asymmetric Inflation Expectations

I use regional survey data to examine how the distribution of inflation expectations relates to inflation during the Great Recession and the post-COVID period.

Read abstract

The standard New Keynesian Phillips Curve summarizes heterogeneous inflation expectations by their mean. I find that the relevant distributional features vary across macroeconomic regimes and regions. A leftward shift in expectations is associated with higher regional inflation during 2008–2012, while a rightward shift is associated with higher inflation during 2020–2025. The post-COVID rightward shift is driven by metro areas across all regions except North Central.

Presented at the Federal Reserve Banks of Philadelphia and Cleveland, Southern Economic Association, and Lewis University.

WORKING PAPER

Heterogeneous Grocery Price Inflation: Online Retail Transformation and Monetary Policy

With Xudong An and Rodney Ramcharan

We study differences in grocery inflation across household incomes using U.S. retail transaction data, including the roles of online shopping, warehouse clubs, and monetary policy.

WORKING PAPER

From Regional to Aggregate: Trade Openness and the Phillips Curve Break After 2000

I examine whether trade exposure contributed to the flattening of the U.S. Phillips curve after 2000 using regional data and a shift-share approach.
